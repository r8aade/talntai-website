How Much Does an AI Receptionist Cost in 2026?

If you run a small or medium business, you've probably weighed three options for covering your phone: hire someone, pay an answering service, or try one of the new AI phone agents. Here's what each actually costs, and what drives the difference.

Hiring a front-desk employee

A part-time front-desk hire in the US typically runs $15-22/hour once you count wages alone — before payroll taxes, training time, turnover, and the fact that they can't answer at 9pm or on a sick day. For most small operators, that's the most expensive option per call actually answered, especially outside business hours.

A traditional answering service

Human answering services usually charge $200-600/month for a base tier, then bill per minute or per call once you go over your plan. They're reliable for basic message-taking, but the person answering usually doesn't know your specific services, pricing, or scheduling — so most calls still get relayed back to you rather than resolved on the spot.

Do-it-yourself AI phone tools

Several AI voice platforms let you build your own phone agent starting around $50-150/month in software costs. That's the cheapest sticker price — but you're the one writing the scripts, connecting your calendar, testing edge cases, and fixing it when a caller asks something it wasn't trained for. For a business owner without time to spare, the "cheap" option often costs more in hours than it saves.

A managed AI Front Desk

A done-for-you AI receptionist — configured for your business, connected to your calendar, and maintained for you — typically runs $250-500/month plus a one-time setup fee in the $500-1,000 range. You're paying a premium over the raw software cost for the same reason you'd pay a contractor instead of buying tools yourself: someone else does the setup and keeps it working.

What actually drives the price

  • Channels covered — phone only is cheaper than phone + text + web chat.
  • Call volume — most plans include a monthly minute allotment, with overage charges beyond it.
  • Integrations — booking directly into your calendar or CRM costs more to set up than a simple message-taker.
  • Ongoing changes — updating hours, pricing, or scripts as your business changes is often what separates a cheap plan from a maintained one.

The real comparison

The honest way to compare isn't sticker price — it's cost per lead actually captured. A $95/month answering service that fails to book half your calls into your calendar is more expensive than a $297/month AI agent that books almost all of them, once you count the jobs you would have won.
